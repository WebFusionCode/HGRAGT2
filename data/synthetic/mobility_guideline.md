# Purpose and scope

This fictional Northstar Health guideline describes the local workflow for planning assisted transfers. It is a demonstration source only and is not a real clinical protocol. Staff must follow current local policy, the device manufacturer's instructions, and the responsible clinician's assessment.

# Before a transfer

The responsible care team records the planned transfer method in the local care plan and checks that the selected equipment is appropriate for the task. Confirm that the equipment is within its inspection date, has no visible damage, and has the correct safe working load for the planned use. A second staff member should be present when the care plan or the equipment instructions require one.

Explain the planned movement to the person before starting and ask whether they are ready. Check that the route is clear, the receiving surface is stable, and brakes are applied where the equipment instructions require them. Staff must stop and reassess if the person's condition, level of assistance, or equipment needs differ from the documented plan.

# Choosing equipment

Use only equipment listed in the current care plan and within the staff member's training. Do not infer that a device is appropriate from its appearance alone. If the care plan does not identify a transfer method, pause and contact the responsible clinician for assessment before proceeding.

The equipment inventory is an operational record, not proof that a device is clean, inspected, or suitable for an individual transfer. Staff must complete the point-of-use checks even when an inventory entry says a device is available.

# Cleaning and equipment status

Follow the device manual and the current transfer equipment contamination response SOP for cleaning. If the instructions conflict, stop use and contact Infection Prevention or the equipment lead for direction. Do not return equipment to service based only on an inventory status.

# Documentation and escalation

Record the equipment identifier, the inspection outcome, and any maintenance or cleaning action in the approved operational record. Do not include patient names, medical record numbers, or other direct identifiers in free-text incident notes. If equipment is damaged, overdue for inspection, or has an uncertain status, label it out of service and notify the equipment lead.

# Preparing the environment

The staff member coordinating a transfer checks the destination and the route before equipment is brought to the bedside. Remove movable obstacles, secure loose cables, and confirm that the receiving surface can be positioned as described in the care plan. Where a privacy screen is used, it must not block access to the equipment controls or the staff member's view of the route.

Keep equipment parked in the designated storage location until it is needed. Do not leave a lift, sling, or transfer aid in a corridor, exit route, or shared treatment area. If the assigned storage point is unavailable, contact the unit equipment lead for an approved temporary location. Do not attach equipment to a bed, trolley, or rail unless the device instructions explicitly allow that attachment.

# Confirming staff readiness

Only staff who have completed the local training for a device may operate it. Before beginning, team members confirm who will operate the controls, who will communicate with the person, and who will observe the route and destination. A staff member must not operate unfamiliar equipment by copying another person's technique.

If a required team member is unavailable, pause and arrange coverage. The transfer must not proceed on the assumption that a second person can be called after movement has started. If the care plan calls for assistance from a specific discipline, contact that team and document any approved change to the plan.

# Communication and consent

Use clear, ordinary language to explain the sequence of the planned transfer. Ask the person how they prefer to receive instructions and whether they need an interpreter or communication aid. Provide time for questions and agreement before movement begins. A person may ask to pause; staff should stop when it is safe to do so and reassess the plan.

Do not make promises about a transfer being risk free. Explain relevant steps and available choices without placing responsibility for equipment selection on the person. If there is uncertainty about whether the plan is understood, pause and ask the responsible clinician to clarify the approach.

# Equipment inspection and selection

Match the device identification label to the equipment requested in the care plan. Check that the safe working load is visible and that any required sling size or attachment configuration is specified. An unreadable label is a reason to remove equipment from use until the equipment lead verifies its identity and status.

Look for tears, pulled stitching, deformed clips, loose fasteners, damaged wheels, missing guards, or unusual noise. Do not attempt an informal repair or replace a component with a part from another device. Isolate the equipment and report the issue through the maintenance route listed by the unit.

Inspection results are point in time observations. A previous inspection entry does not replace a fresh check before use. If the equipment was moved, stored improperly, exposed to contamination, or used in a way not described by its instructions, reassess its status and follow the current escalation process.

# During a planned transfer

Follow the sequence in the current care plan and the device manual. Keep hands clear of moving components and use the designated controls. Do not alter equipment settings or attachments during movement unless the instructions describe the change and the operator is trained to perform it.

Pause if the equipment behaves unexpectedly, a person reports new discomfort, a route becomes blocked, or the team cannot maintain the planned communication. Return to a stable position if that can be done safely, then contact the responsible clinician or equipment lead. Do not continue merely to complete the planned task.

Staff should remain attentive to the whole route rather than focusing only on the device controls. Confirm that clothing, bedding, and other items cannot catch in moving parts. At the destination, stabilize the equipment before releasing attachments and return the device to its prescribed safe position.

# After the transfer

Make sure the person is settled and that any agreed call system or communication aid is within reach. Tell the care team if the transfer differed from the documented plan or if equipment did not operate as expected. Record an incident or near miss using the approved reporting process, keeping direct identifiers out of general notes.

Return reusable equipment to the designated cleaning location after use. Follow the device manual and the active contamination SOP for the required process. Keep clean and unprocessed equipment physically separated where the unit workflow provides separate locations. Do not mark equipment available until the required cleaning, inspection, and documentation steps are complete.

# Accessibility and individual preferences

The care team records communication preferences and any agreed transfer considerations in the approved care plan. Staff should consult that plan before each transfer and confirm whether the information remains applicable. Do not infer an individual's abilities or preferences from a diagnosis, age, appearance, or a previous transfer.

Where accessible equipment or a communication aid is required but unavailable, pause and contact the responsible clinician or unit lead. Do not substitute an unapproved device solely because it is nearby. Operational staff may identify available equipment but do not determine whether it is clinically appropriate for a person.

# Service disruption and unavailable equipment

If the equipment named in the care plan is unavailable, notify the responsible clinician and equipment lead. They assess whether another approved device and an updated plan are available. The unit inventory is a location aid and must not be treated as authorization to substitute equipment.

During a maintenance outage, follow the local downtime procedure. Keep affected devices labeled and separated from available equipment. Record the asset identifier, date, reason for removal, and the team notified. Only the equipment lead or an authorized delegate may update an asset to available after required checks are complete.

# Learning and audit

Unit leads review a sample of equipment inspection records, cleaning entries, maintenance notices, and incident reports at the interval set by the local governance schedule. The review checks that entries are complete, traceable to an equipment identifier, and free of direct personal identifiers in general text fields.

Audit findings are shared with the relevant operational and clinical owners. Repeated delays, missing records, or unclear handoffs should be referred to the policy owner for process review. This guideline does not set an individual performance target or replace local incident review.

Staff feedback about unclear instructions should be sent to the Clinical Practice Council. Until an approved revision is published, use the current effective version and contact the named owner when instructions cannot be reconciled.
